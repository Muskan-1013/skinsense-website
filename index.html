The oiliness reading

Why blotting paper and light?

The proposed method compares light reflected from clean paper with paper after it contacts the skin. Oil can change the paper's appearance. The BH1750 measures light levels, so the result is an estimate, not a clinical sebum test.

Science & Accuracy.

Calibration plan

  • Moisture: compare dry-skin and wet-skin references.
  • pH: use buffer references around pH 4.0 and 9.0.
  • Oiliness: compare clean paper with oil-saturated paper.

Why these readings?

Moisture, pH, and surface oil can provide context about how skin feels and behaves. Readings can vary with placement, pressure, temperature, products, and calibration.

What your readings mean

A single measurement cannot determine skin type or sensitivity. pH readings alone do not prove that skin is sensitive. Consider repeated readings as general context and seek professional advice about persistent symptoms.

For dry-feeling skin
